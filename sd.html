System design & low-level design

Nine worked designs, five of them taken directly from the interview experiences on this site. Each has requirements, a walkthrough, a code or schema sketch, the deep-dive follow-ups with answers, and a drilling chain showing where candidates break. Before that: how much of this actually applies at your level, the 45-minute framework, the numbers, the building blocks and LLD.

Read this first if you are SDE-1

No SDE-1 loop in the corpus had a standalone system design round. Design still appeared — but inside coding rounds, in three forms:

  • A 20–25 minute "design a data structure that supports X" question in the middle of a technical round — the post-office hierarchy problem, which is the first worked design below.
  • "Implement a cache with get() and set()" in a Bar Raiser round.
  • A round where the interviewer announced two DSA questions and then spent 40 of 60 minutes on the candidate's own project — architecture, data storage choices, verification logic, edge cases, scalability, failure scenarios. That is a design interview under a different name, and it is the one you are most likely to face.

So: do the two SDE-1 designs, read the framework and the LLD section, and then rehearse your own project as a design walkthrough using the 45-minute structure. The large HLD designs are worth reading if you have time, and are what you would face at SDE-2.
