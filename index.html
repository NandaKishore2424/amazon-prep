What an in-person hiring event is

Amazon India runs periodic in-person hiring events — bulk interview drives where they book office space and run many candidates' full loops over a few days, usually at Bangalore or Hyderabad. If you are invited to one, the interest form asks two separate location questions that are easy to confuse:

Preferred Location 1 / 2
Where the job would be. Team location.
The in-person event question
Where the interview happens. Not the same thing — you can prefer Chennai for the role and still interview in Bangalore.
The venue choice
Which city you would travel to for the interview itself. They typically run the event at more than one site.
A "tentative" window
The stated range is the window, not your date. You get one specific day and slot inside it, confirmed later.

Four things worth knowing

  • Your whole loop is compressed into one day. For SDE-1 that is typically 2–3 back-to-back 45–60 min rounds, each carrying both DSA and Leadership Principles, one of them a Bar Raiser with veto power.
  • Saying yes is not a shortlist confirmation. It is an availability survey used to size the event and pick venues.
  • Ask the recruiter about travel and accommodation before booking anything. It is usually covered for out-of-station candidates at these drives, but confirm in writing.
  • In-person often means whiteboard or paper, not an IDE. At least one experience in the corpus explicitly warns about this. Hand-write a few solutions before you go.

Worth asking the recruiter for: the exact date once locked, the number and type of rounds, and the travel policy.

What the corpus says, in one place

  • Leadership Principles decide outcomes. One SDE-II in Bangalore was rejected purely on an average "Customer Obsession" rating after clean technical rounds. One SDE-1 called the LP round the hardest of the day — 40 minutes of poker-faced follow-ups.
  • You do not need perfect submissions. One candidate passed 8/13 and 2/9 test cases and still advanced, because the logic was right. Another never reached optimal DP on Word Break and was still selected.
  • The project round is real. In the Feb 2026 SDE-1 loop the interviewer announced two DSA questions, then spent 40 of 60 minutes on the candidate's project — architecture, storage choices, edge cases, failure scenarios, scaling.
  • DP and trees are over-represented. House Robber, Binary Tree Cameras, Word Break, Target Sum, matrix path DP, maximum path sum, tree views and traversals all appear.
  • State complexity every single time. Every experience that lists tips mentions it.
  • Use your last five minutes. The L5 candidate who got the offer spent them asking about on-call culture and patent policy in every round.

Where to start

In priority order. The syllabus pages are built to be worked through properly; when time is short, use the filters on each to hit only what the corpus shows actually gets asked.

  1. Leadership Principles, end to end. Then use the story matrix to write 8–10 STAR stories covering all 16. This is where people actually get cut, and it is the only part you cannot cram at the last minute.
  2. DSA sheet filtered to ★ asked, then the Core tier of Arrays, Hashing, Binary Search, Trees and DP. Run the DP ladder on House Robber out loud, on paper.
  3. Rehearse your own project as a design walkthrough using the 45-minute framework — architecture, storage, edge cases, what broke, what you would change. In the corpus one interviewer spent 40 of 60 minutes on exactly this.
  4. Fundamentals, core-filtered only: OS modules 4 and 5 (concurrency, memory) and DBMS modules 4 and 5 (indexing, transactions). Those four are what the corpus shows being asked live.
  5. The night before: re-read your STAR stories, not more LeetCode. Sleep.
